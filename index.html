This check follows the air through the home: fans that should vent outside, windows and vents that should open, the moisture you can measure, and radon, which you can only find by testing. You look, measure and record. You never take covers off or change a vent fitted for a fuel appliance.

Before you start - safety first

HSE
If a CO alarm sounds: this is not a ventilation check. Leave the home immediately and call your local emergency number. Seek prompt medical attention if anyone feels dizzy, light-headed or sick. (CDC Carbon Monoxide)
  • Look only. Never take fan covers off, open electrical parts or unblock a vent fitted for a fuel-burning appliance. (Professional practice)
  • Roof space: look from the hatch only, with a steady ladder and someone with you. Never step between joists. (Professional practice)
  • Pre-Purchase: ask before switching fans on, opening windows or placing a humidity meter. (Professional practice)

Humidity Readings

SHEET 04 ·

US EPA: keep indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. Use a simple digital hygrometer. Take readings in the same rooms at different times (for example, first thing in the morning and after a shower or cooking) so you can see the pattern.

Radon Test Record

Radon is a natural radioactive gas you cannot see or smell. The only way to know the level is to test. WHO: measure over at least 3 months for an annual average. Enter the result exactly as printed on the lab report, in Bq/m³ or pCi/L (1 pCi/L = 37 Bq/m³).

LocationTestDatesResultCompared with

National radon action levels differ. Examples: US EPA fix at 4 pCi/L (about 150 Bq/m³) or higher, consider fixing between 2 and 4 pCi/L; WHO reference level 100 Bq/m³. Always follow your own country's radon guidance.

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It does not measure airflow rates, pollutants or radon. Only instruments, laboratory tests and qualified specialists can do that.
    • It makes no medical claims and does not link symptoms to the home. Health concerns belong with a doctor.
    • It does not confirm compliance with building regulations, which differ by country.
    • It never asks you to open electrical parts, remove fan covers or change a vent fitted for a fuel-burning appliance.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Guidance used in this tool

    • Humidity: keep indoor humidity below 60 percent (ideally between 30 and 50 percent) relative humidity. (EPA Mold & Moisture)
    • Extraction: run the bathroom fan or open the window when showering; use exhaust fans or open windows when cooking or dishwashing; vent clothes dryers, stoves and kerosene heaters to the outside where possible. (EPA Mold & Moisture)
    • Fans that exhaust outdoors: local bathroom or kitchen fans that exhaust outdoors remove contaminants directly from the room and increase the outdoor air ventilation rate. For most indoor air problems at home, source control is the most effective solution. (EPA Improving IAQ)
    • Too little outdoor air: pollutants can accumulate. Sources include fuel-burning appliances, tobacco products, building materials, furnishings and household products. (EPA Intro to IAQ)
    • UK guidance: extractor fans linked to the outside (kitchen fans preferably near the hob); humidity-controlled or over-run fans; trickle vents controllable by the occupant; windows on the vent position where feasible; open windows wide after cooking or bathing; cook with lids on; dry laundry outdoors where possible; keep a gap between furniture and external walls. (UK Gov Damp & Mould)
    • Radon: US EPA: fix at 4 pCi/L (about 150 Bq/m³) or higher; consider fixing between 2 and 4 pCi/L. WHO: national reference level of 100 Bq/m³ (up to 300 where 100 is not possible); measure over at least 3 months. (EPA Radon, WHO Radon)
    • Units: 1 pCi/L = 37 Bq/m³. Relative humidity in %. Metric first, imperial in brackets.
    Country-specific: trickle vent and extractor fan requirements quoted here come from UK guidance and building regulations. Radon action levels, radon maps and testing schemes differ by country. Check your national building regulations and radon agency.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.
